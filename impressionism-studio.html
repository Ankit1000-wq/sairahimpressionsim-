Art History

What is Impressionism?

Born in Paris in 1874, Impressionism was a revolution in art — capturing the fleeting beauty of the everyday world through light, color, and feeling.

☀️

Light & Color

Impressionists were obsessed with natural light. They painted the same scene at different times of day to show how light transforms everything — a haystack at noon looks nothing like at sunset.

🖌️

Loose Brushstrokes

Instead of blending smoothly, Impressionists applied dabs of pure color side-by-side. Up close it looks rough — from a distance, the eye blends them into vibrant, living scenes.

☕

Everyday Life

Rather than grand historical scenes, Impressionists painted ordinary moments: people at cafés, children playing, dancers in sunlight. Everyday life itself was worthy of art.

The Masters

Claude Monet
1840–1926

The father of Impressionism. Monet painted his famous Water Lilies while almost blind — their dreamy haze came from his cataracts. He created 250 Water Lily paintings.

Signature Colors
Pierre-Auguste Renoir
1841–1919

Renoir painted the joy of life. His portraits radiate warmth — figures glow as if lit from within. He created over 6,000 paintings and married a woman he painted.

Signature Colors
Camille Pissarro
1830–1903

The "dean of the Impressionists" — the only artist to show in all 8 Impressionist exhibitions. He mentored Cézanne and Gauguin, painting rural life and Parisian boulevards.

Signature Colors
